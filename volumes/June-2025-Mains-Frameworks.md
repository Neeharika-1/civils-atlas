# June 2025 · Original Mains answer laboratory

Practice outlines, not previous-year questions or predicted answers. Use historical evidence with its date; verify later law and implementation. AP applications are analytical suggestions, not evidence that the state implemented every measure.

## 1. Monetary easing with a neutral stance · 250 words
Prompt: A central bank can reduce the policy rate while remaining neutral. Explain with the June decision.
Opening: Identify the 50-basis-point cut to 5.5% and distinguish rate from stance.
Mechanism: Policy signal → market funding costs → bank deposit/lending repricing → consumption and investment, with variable lags.
Balance: Transmission depends on liquidity, loan benchmarks, credit demand and bank balance sheets. Inflation risks and global shocks can justify flexibility rather than a promise of further cuts.
AP application: Discuss agricultural/MSME cash-flow sensitivity without inventing a state growth effect.
Conclusion: Judge easing by transmitted financial conditions and balanced macro outcomes, not the announcement alone.
Study references: E5-2506-001; February and April MPC notes; August pause.

## 2. Credible census and public trust · 250 words
Prompt: Census quality depends as much on governance as on technology. Discuss.
Opening: Separate June announcement, future enumeration and reference dates; the general announced date was March 2027.
Mechanism: Clear definitions → trained enumeration → privacy and validation → usable aggregates for planning.
Balance: Exclusion, ambiguous classifications, digital access and politicised interpretation can undermine credibility. Caste totals are not automatically reservation decisions.
Safeguards: Accessible collection, community communication, secure handling, independent quality checks and transparent metadata.
Conclusion: A trustworthy statistical foundation needs methodological consistency and accountable administration.
Study reference: E5-2506-003.

## 3. Localisation versus assembly labels · 250 words
Prompt: Evaluate conditional industrial incentives using semiconductor SEZs and electric-car manufacturing.
Opening: Identify the sector-specific land relaxation and conditional import route, not universal concessions.
Mechanism: Lower entry constraints → committed investment → supplier development → verifiable domestic value addition.
Balance: Infrastructure, skills, standards and supplier learning take time; imported assembly and announcements do not establish an ecosystem. Incentives have fiscal and competitive costs.
AP application: Evaluate ports, power, land rights, skill pipelines and public milestone disclosure for proposed investments.
Conclusion: Use value-chain capability and additional investment, with credible verification, rather than the count of approvals.
Study references: E5-2506-004, E5-2506-005.

## 4. Regulating secured and project credit · 250 words
Prompt: Prudential differentiation is more effective than uniform rules. Discuss using gold loans and project finance.
Opening: Collateral and future cash flows generate different risks; LTV and commercial-operation dates answer different questions.
Mechanism: Appropriate limits and monitoring → lower excessive exposure → better incentives and early recognition of stress.
Balance: Overly restrictive implementation can exclude viable borrowers; lax valuation or repeated delays can hide losses. Directions have applicability dates and category conditions.
Evaluation: Track borrower costs, loan performance, valuation quality and legitimate project-delay treatment.
Conclusion: Predictable, proportionate rules should protect lenders and borrowers without pretending collateral eliminates risk.
Study references: E5-2506-007, E5-2506-008.

## 5. Restoration beyond tree counts · 250 words
Prompt: Forest and farm-tree policies require different but complementary safeguards.
Opening: Contrast ecological restoration under Green India with facilitation of agroforestry harvesting.
Mechanism: Suitable species and landscapes → survival and ecosystem recovery; simpler traceable farm-tree rules → farmer incentives and legal wood supply.
Balance: Monocultures, unsuitable planting and weak traceability can undermine biodiversity or facilitate laundering. Model rules need lawful state implementation.
AP application: Distinguish natural forest, dryland ecosystems and farm trees; incorporate local livelihoods and water availability.
Conclusion: Measure ecosystem function and sustained farmer returns, not only seedlings, hectares or permission speed.
Study references: E5-2506-014, E5-2506-015.

## 6. Talliki Vandanam and accountable transfers · 250 words
Prompt: Cash-transfer evaluation must distinguish entitlement, payment and outcomes. Discuss with AP’s education support.
Opening: State the ₹15,000 gross and ₹13,000 after the general maintenance deduction, before category-specific RTE/scholarship adjustments.
Mechanism: Lower household financial barriers → continued attendance and education participation, subject to service quality and eligibility design.
Balance: Exclusion errors, delayed credits and unclear deductions can reduce trust. Transfer receipt is not proof of improved learning; school maintenance expenditure needs separate accounts.
Evaluation: Eligible-to-paid reconciliation, accessible appeals, attendance, transitions and learning, with privacy protections.
Conclusion: Transparent benefit communication and functioning schools determine whether a transfer improves capabilities.
Study reference: E5-2506-018.

## 7. Crowd safety as a governance duty · 150 words
Prompt: Crowd disasters should not be explained only through individual behaviour.
Opening: Distinguish attendance, density and dangerous crowd movement.
Mechanism: Capacity controls, safe ingress/egress, real-time information and clear command reduce pressure points.
Balance: Event approvals without tested operational plans do little. Victim blaming obscures design failures, misinformation and fragmented responsibility.
Way forward: Independent safety review, accessible exits, trained stewards, coordinated health response and transparent post-incident learning.
Conclusion: Treat crowd safety as a shared institutional duty with identifiable accountability.
Study reference: E5-2506-024.

## 8. Scientific milestones and evidentiary restraint · 250 words
Prompt: Scientific ambition requires careful communication of what a milestone proves.
Opening: Contrast BharatFS resolution, Axiom-4 participation and a preliminary aviation report.
Mechanism: Higher model detail supports forecasting; mission experience supports research; preliminary investigation organises early evidence.
Balance: None establishes perfect village forecasts, indigenous crewed-launch readiness or final causal blame. Date adoption, launch, return and publication separately.
AP application: Weather advisories should communicate uncertainty and actionable lead times, while space participation should be evaluated through validated research and transferable capability.
Conclusion: Specific evidence strengthens public trust more than sweeping achievement or failure claims.
Study references: E5-2506-025, E5-2506-026, E5-2506-031.

# August 2025 · Original Mains answer laboratory

Historical practice, not a certification of current law, later judicial outcomes or examination predictions. Cite the correct legislative stage and dated evidence.

## 1. Integrity in office and the presumption of innocence · 250 words
Prompt: Critically examine custody-based removal proposals for ministers.
Opening: The 130th Amendment proposal was introduced and referred to a Joint Committee, not already enacted in August.
Mechanism: A specified period of custody for serious offences would trigger ministerial-office consequences; conviction and legislative disqualification remain different questions.
Balance: Integrity and functional governance matter, but arrest can be misused. Federal distribution of investigative authority and the timing of detention affect fairness.
Safeguards: Independent review, precise conditions, procedural transparency and attention to alternatives.
Conclusion: Institutional integrity should not make untested allegations an easy tool for displacing elected government.
Study reference: E5-2508-009.

## 2. AI in finance: innovation with contestable decisions · 250 words
Prompt: Responsible financial AI needs governance across its lifecycle.
Opening: FREE-AI is a committee report; recommendations are not all automatically binding rules.
Mechanism: Appropriate data, documented models, testing and monitoring → useful decisions → accessible review and correction.
Balance: Accuracy can conceal group bias, data leakage, opaque reasoning and vulnerability to attacks. Human oversight must be competent, not ceremonial.
AP application: Offer meaningful complaints and assisted access when automated fraud/credit decisions affect users.
Way forward: Proportionate audits, accountable deployment, security testing, transparency and remedies.
Conclusion: Trust depends on explainable responsibility and correction, not the label “AI-powered”.
Study references: E5-2508-007, E5-2508-012; July audit guidance.

## 3. Online gaming and proportionate classification · 250 words
Prompt: Evaluate the challenge of protecting users while preserving legitimate digital play.
Opening: The August framework distinguishes money gaming from qualifying e-sports and social games.
Mechanism: Define prohibited stakes/expected monetary enrichment → address advertising/payment facilitation → enforce workable boundaries.
Balance: Skill alone does not settle the defined money-gaming category. Subscription fees, tournament conditions and wagers need legal analysis; overbroad claims can misinform consumers and firms.
Safeguards: Clear definitions, proportionate implementation, review and attention to later rules/litigation.
Conclusion: Protect users through precise law and credible enforcement rather than assuming all gaming is identical.
Study reference: E5-2508-024.

## 4. AP women’s mobility and operational sustainability · 250 words
Prompt: Fare-free transport should be evaluated through agency and service quality, not uptake alone.
Opening: Stree Shakti launched on 15 August for eligible passengers and qualifying service categories.
Mechanism: Lower trip costs → wider access to work, education, care and public services, if routes and schedules match needs.
Balance: Crowding, reimbursement delays and insufficient fleet capacity can reduce benefits. Zero passenger fare is not zero operating cost.
Evaluation: Trip purposes, waiting time, safety, service reliability, distributional access and audited reimbursements.
Conclusion: Sustainable public finance and reliable capacity are complements to expanded mobility rights.
Study reference: E5-2508-030.

## 5. Farm transfers, public attribution and inclusion · 250 words
Prompt: Assess income support using Annadata Sukhibhava–PM Kisan.
Opening: Distinguish the announced ₹20,000 annual combined package from the ₹7,000 first instalment; separate Centre and state shares.
Mechanism: Timely predictable support can smooth seasonal liquidity and consumption.
Balance: Eligibility based on records can exclude genuine cultivators; tenant-farmer treatment must be checked in operative rules. Transfers do not replace productive public investment or risk management.
Evaluation: Eligible-to-paid reconciliation, appeals, timing, farmer-category coverage and fiscal sustainability.
Conclusion: Transparent attribution and inclusion make a transfer more credible, without overstating its power to solve structural farm distress.
Study reference: E5-2508-029.

## 6. Trade shock resilience for AP exporters · 250 words
Prompt: How should a state respond to a sudden external tariff shock?
Opening: The August US headline reached 50% for many covered products; exemptions and later changes require verification.
Mechanism: Higher landed cost → bargaining and margin pressure → possible demand/sourcing adjustment, varying by product.
Balance: Exporters, importers and consumers can share incidence. Market diversification needs standards, logistics and buyers; it is not instantaneous or costless.
AP application: Use seafood as an exposure channel, without inventing realised loss, job or export figures.
Way forward: Product-level intelligence, standards support, trade finance, value addition and adjustment assistance.
Conclusion: Pair national diplomacy with evidence-led firm adaptation.
Study references: E5-2508-031; CA-033; MY25-002.

## 7. Sustainable port-led development · 250 words
Prompt: Maritime legal reform is necessary but insufficient for competitive coastal logistics.
Opening: Distinguish merchant shipping, coastal trading and port governance; non-major is an administrative category, not a size label.
Mechanism: Clear registration/licensing and dispute rules → greater predictability; hinterland links and reliable services → usable logistics gains.
Balance: Cargo economics, community impacts, marine ecology and climate resilience can constrain outcomes. Passage is not operational completion.
AP application: Assess state maritime coordination, connectivity and coastal livelihoods rather than infer benefit from coastline length.
Conclusion: Legal clarity should be complemented by commercially viable and ecologically responsible implementation.
Study references: E5-2508-016, E5-2508-017, E5-2508-018; July bills of lading.

## 8. Mountain disasters and evidence-led development · 250 words
Prompt: Disaster resilience requires more than labelling an event a cloudburst.
Opening: Separate intense-rainfall observation, flood mechanism and the disaster’s impacts.
Mechanism: Hazard interacts with slope instability, drainage, land use, infrastructure and exposed settlements.
Balance: Sparse gauges can limit event classification; individual climate attribution needs analysis. Avoid treating all Himalayan floods as the same process.
Action: Better observations, warnings, evacuation planning, safe siting and cumulative development assessment; use repeated satellite data where validated.
Conclusion: Reduce vulnerability while improving causal evidence, rather than substitute dramatic terminology for preparedness.
Study references: E5-2508-033; E5-2507-027; E5-2506-026.

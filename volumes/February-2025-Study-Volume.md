## 1. Can a lower fiscal deficit coexist with development spending? · 250 words
**Opening:** Distinguish fiscal consolidation from indiscriminate expenditure reduction. Use the Union Budget’s 4.4% FY2025–26 fiscal-deficit estimate as a dated proposal.
**Build:** Explain revenue mobilisation, interest costs, expenditure composition, capital investment and medium-term debt sustainability. State why the quality and execution of spending matter more than allocation headlines.
**Counterpoint:** Cutting productive investment or essential welfare can weaken growth and future revenue; contingent liabilities can hide risk outside the headline deficit.
**AP application:** Compare budget estimates with revised estimates, state receipts, borrowing and capital-project execution. Central capex loans are liabilities even when terms are concessional.
**Conclude:** Credible assumptions, transparent liabilities, outcome evaluation and protection of high-return services reconcile fiscal prudence with development.
**Avoid:** Equating a budgeted allocation with money spent. References: CA-001, CA-016, FEB25-044–046.

## 2. What makes agricultural diversification economically sustainable? · 250 words
**Opening:** Introduce the Budget’s pulses, cotton, seed and high-value crop proposals as attempts to address productivity and value-chain gaps.
**Build:** Connect suitable agroecology, quality seed, extension, water-use efficiency, procurement confidence, storage, processing and reliable market access. Distinguish diversification of output from guaranteed diversification of income.
**Counterpoint:** A board, scheme or higher credit ceiling cannot remove price volatility, tenant exclusion or climate risk on its own.
**AP application:** Use local crop suitability, horticulture, coastal fisheries and farmer-producer organisations without assuming a Bihar-specific Makhana Board benefits all states directly.
**Conclude:** Encourage region-appropriate production with risk management, value addition and credible market institutions.
**Avoid:** Presenting proposed missions or KCC limits as universal implemented entitlements. References: CA-004–007, FEB25-019–020.

**Avoid:** Assuming poultry outbreaks prove sustained human-to-human transmission. Reference: FEB25-042.

## 7. How should AP evaluate major irrigation and capital projects? · 250 words
**Opening:** Distinguish capital allocations, project construction and actual delivery of benefits.
**Build:** Examine financing, implementation milestones, environmental compliance, command-area readiness, operation and maintenance, and rehabilitation/resettlement.
**Counterpoint:** Procurement progress and completed civil works can coexist with delayed livelihood restoration or last-mile water delivery.
**AP application:** Use dated Polavaram and Amaravati allocations and the rehabilitation tender decision. Identify affected-family outcomes and transparent grievance redress as independent metrics.
**Conclude:** Evaluate projects through delivered services and fair treatment of affected people, supported by public stage-wise reporting.
**Avoid:** Equating a fresh tender with completed resettlement. References: FEB25-046–047.

## 8. How can India evaluate strategic technology progress realistically? · 150 words
**Opening:** Use the CE20 ground test, Euclid’s lensing image and the neutrino announcement to separate different kinds of scientific evidence.
**Build:** Distinguish proof of principle, ground qualification, flight validation, operational deployment and scientific discovery. Explain uncertainty, independent verification and iteration.
**Counterpoint:** Celebratory headlines can collapse mission stages or confuse observation dates with publication dates.
**Conclude:** Support sustained scientific capacity while measuring each programme against its actual technical milestone.
**Avoid:** Claiming the CE20 test proved an in-flight restart, or that the KM3NeT detection occurred in February 2025. References: FEB25-033–036.

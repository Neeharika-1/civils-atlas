#### Money-mule draft: freezing fraud proceeds needs a reviewable process
E16-2609-005 · P1 · 2026-09-11 · National / other

**Why in the news:** RBI’s 11 September release invites comments on draft KYC changes following an August Supreme Court direction. Detailed timelines below are PRS-reviewed proposals, not independently read final SOP.

**Source-backed essentials**
- A suspected transaction or account may face a temporary debit hold, with immediate notice and an opportunity to explain.
- The proposed explanation period is 20 days; review follows within 10 days after an explanation or 30 days from hold if none arrives.
- Banks would need internal policies, technology, communication templates, cyber-fraud portal links and grievance redress; comments close 2 October.

**Static connection · original guidance:** A debit hold differs from seizure, confiscation or a criminal conviction. Account-level restrictions can affect legitimate livelihood receipts.

**Analysis · original guidance**
- Target the suspect amount where justified, maintain reasoned records and ensure timely escalation/removal according to lawful procedure. Automated suspicion scores should not become irrebuttable evidence.
- For AP residents affected by cyber fraud, accessible notice, assisted response and coordination with police can protect victims and innocent recipients. This note does not offer a universal legal release deadline under unread final rules.

## Answer key and explanations

**SEP26-01. B** — Goods, services and income jointly determine the current account. The primary table and release show reserve depletion, not accumulation; release month differs from the observation period. Study reference: E16-2609-001.

**SEP26-02. A** — Gross receipts and a net account balance are distinct. Preserve definitions and do not mix nonreserve financial flows with reserve-inclusive aggregates. Study reference: E16-2609-001.

**SEP26-03. A** — The general rate is 0.4% with a ₹300 cap, reached at ₹75,000. This is merchant MDR subject to category/exemption conditions, not a universal consumer or P2P transfer charge. Study reference: E16-2609-002.

**SEP26-04. D** — The FAQ separates free payer/P2P use from selective merchant pricing. It prohibits merchant passthrough, but this does not prove that indirect economic incidence through general prices is impossible. Study reference: E16-2609-002.

**SEP26-05. C** — The paper discusses related-party voting, avoidance transactions, valuation and creditor reasoning. Comments and later regulatory steps must be distinguished from consultation publication. Study reference: E16-2609-004.

**SEP26-06. B** — The RBI release confirms a consultation; detailed procedures were not independently read as a final SOP. Suspicion, debit restriction and criminal conviction remain different stages. Study reference: E16-2609-005.

**SEP26-07. D** — The same abbreviation can have context-specific meanings. State liquidity support is repayable and distinct from a fiscal grant; the committee’s recommendations were to be examined, not automatically implemented. Study reference: E16-2609-006.

**SEP26-08. C** — The short notification specifies a statutory coverage ceiling and commencement, not every scheme detail. Wage ceiling, contribution rate, basic pay and pension amount are distinct. Study reference: E16-2609-007.

**SEP26-09. B** — Power and energy are different dimensions. Cabinet approval and a target do not establish installed or operating capacity, state allocations or completed spending. Study reference: E16-2609-008.

**SEP26-10. B** — The inspected Gazette gives 1 January 2027. The separate food-laboratory amendments commence 1 April 2027; legal instruments should not be mixed. Study reference: E16-2609-010.

**SEP26-11. C** — A low prior-price reference helps prevent an inflated base price from manufacturing an apparent discount. It must not be replaced with an unspecified notion of old price. Study reference: E16-2609-010.

**SEP26-12. A** — MSP announcement, procurement access and realised prices differ. The official cost footnote uses paid-out costs plus family labour, not automatically C2. Study reference: E16-2609-013.

**SEP26-13. A** — The DAC headline concerns about ₹1.10 lakh crore of proposals. The planned Indian-industry procurement share is not identical to domestic content in every component. Study reference: E16-2609-014.

**SEP26-14. C** — The inspected passage distinguishes 14-day regulatory/referral reporting and five-day import reporting, with reasons for delay. Scientific validity and legal procedure remain essential. Study reference: E16-2609-016.

**SEP26-15. C** — Parcel identification can link records, but does not automatically adjudicate title, remove errors or extinguish other rights. Study reference: E16-2609-018.

**SEP26-16. C** — Strategic capability requires the entire value chain. Historical magazine review should not silently change an approval’s date. Study reference: E16-2609-022.

**SEP26-17. A** — Off-chain records, legally enforceable claims, custody, redemption, tax and regulatory jurisdiction remain important. Technical settlement does not equal unquestionable ownership. Study reference: E16-2609-023.

**SEP26-18. D** — Botanical classification and legal definitions are distinct. Location, rights, species and supply-chain design matter for lawful and sustainable livelihoods. Study reference: E16-2609-025.

**SEP26-19. A** — The Montreal framework and Kigali connect ozone transition with climate protection, but the physical problems and phasedown obligations must be distinguished. Study reference: E16-2609-029.

**SEP26-20. D** — Geometry, slope, fractures and bed conditions influence hazards. An ice avalanche, GLOF and rain-driven flood are different initiating processes that may cascade. Study reference: E16-2609-032.

**SEP26-21. D** — The far side receives sunlight. The 21 cm rest transition is redshifted; calibration and foregrounds remain challenges even with radio shielding. Study reference: E16-2609-036.

**SEP26-22. A** — Different serovars and syndromes require careful interpretation. A study’s case definition and population cannot be silently changed when reporting its burden estimate. Study reference: E16-2609-038.

**SEP26-23. B** — A draft policy or discussion is not a site allotment. Water quality, waste, disease, common-water use and participation need explicit safeguards. Study reference: E16-2609-045.

**SEP26-24. B** — DBT describes a payment mechanism, not automatic universal entitlement or an audited receipt. The reviewed news report did not provide original rules or bank-level verification. Study reference: E16-2609-047.

**SEP26-25. A** — A guarantee may be called if specified obligations fail. Borrowing, disbursement, project operation and actual state payment are different stages and should be disclosed separately. Study reference: E16-2609-050.

**SEP26-26. D** — The reported Cabinet decision has a limited extent. Reclassification is distinct from allotment, possession and safe construction; the original GO and survey were not independently inspected. Study reference: E16-2609-051.

**SEP26-27. B** — The 23 September Cabinet report identifies four cities. Approval does not itself establish final boundaries, commencement, staffing or improved crime outcomes. Study reference: E16-2609-052.

**SEP26-28. B** — The ₹22,177.84 crore figure is an approval pipeline. Financial closure, construction, production and sustained work must be separately tracked; unreconciled component totals should not be invented. Study reference: E16-2609-054.

**SEP26-29. D** — The increase is ₹644.98 crore, approximately 71.35% of ₹904 crore. CIIP here concerns Amaravati capital-area panchayat infrastructure, not an irrigation project. Arithmetic does not establish the causes of escalation or completed spending; original revised estimates remain unread. Study reference: E16-2609-056.

**SEP26-30. C** — Requests, orders, procurement, arrival and retail sale are separate stages. Stage and unit errors are serious even when the headline quantity is remembered. Study reference: E16-2609-058.


# September 2026 · Original Mains answer frameworks

Eight frameworks for 150/250-word practice; not model claims of current law or exhaustive coverage. Preserve consultation and implementation stages.

## 1. Digital payments need a sustainable funding model without weakening inclusion.
Study anchor: E16-2609-002 · UPI MDR: merchant pricing is not a consumer transfer fee

1. Anchor the 15 September official FAQ and future 15 October commencement. Separate eligible merchant MDR from free ordinary payer/P2P use.
2. Explain the general 0.4% rate, ₹300 cap and exemptions/special categories without applying them indiscriminately to credit-linked products.
3. Analyse resilience, acceptance costs, merchant categorisation, pass-through incentives, competition and small-business margins. No-surcharge rules do not prove indirect economic incidence is impossible.
4. Propose transparent pricing, assisted classification, complaint resolution, reliable infrastructure and review using actual transaction/merchant outcomes.
5. For AP small merchants, test whether compliance knowledge, access and payment reliability improve rather than equating digital transaction growth with universal inclusion.

## 2. Renewable expansion is a system-integration challenge, not just a generation target.
Study anchor: E16-2609-008 · Green Energy Corridor III: integrate generation, transmission and storage

1. Anchor GEC III approval on 30 September: headline ₹1,86,405 crore, central support ₹54,082 crore, up to 135 GW evacuation and 50 GWh BESS through FY2032–33.
2. Distinguish power, stored energy, annual output, grid connection and operating service. Withhold unreconciled component sums rather than manufacturing precision.
3. Analyse transmission congestion, right-of-way, variable output, storage duration, degradation, safety and cost allocation.
4. Propose coordinated resource planning, competitive procurement where applicable, transparent milestones and performance monitoring tied to reliability and usable renewables.
5. For AP, link DISCOM finances, industrial/agricultural demand and project pipelines without asserting a new state allocation or already commissioned corridor.

## 3. Digital-market consumer protection requires enforceable design standards and informed choice.
Study anchor: E16-2609-010 · E-commerce amendment: notification now, commencement in 2027

1. Anchor the 9 September notification and 1 January 2027 commencement; contrast notification with operative effect.
2. Explain sponsored-ranking transparency, the lowest-prior-30-day discount reference and annual dark-pattern audit requirements.
3. Analyse information asymmetry, platform power, conflicted ranking, seller dependence, complaint evidence and audit credibility.
4. Propose independent enforcement capability, clear disclosures, accessible redress and proportionate sanctions while preserving genuine innovation and small-seller access.
5. For AP, assess whether consumer complaints and seller experiences improve; avoid assuming every online offer is unlawful or every self-audit is reliable.

## 4. Integrated land information must be accurate, contestable and inclusive.
Study anchor: E16-2609-018 · DILRMP3.0: a LandStack does not adjudicate every title

1. Anchor DILRMP 3.0’s September roadmap and primary anticipated ₹565.50 crore national outlay, retaining the prelaunch versus launch distinction.
2. Distinguish a 14-digit parcel identifier, map, mutation, registered transaction and adjudicated title; digitisation is not automatic conclusive ownership.
3. Analyse legacy discrepancies, survey quality, interdepartmental responsibility, privacy, exclusion and access barriers for women, tenants and customary users.
4. Propose notices, assisted claims, ground verification, audit trails, time-bound correction and meaningful appeal alongside interoperable technology.
5. For AP, measure fewer erroneous refusals, resolved disputes and reduced citizen burden rather than only map uploads or assuming a separate newly verified state scheme.

## 5. Public institutions deploying AI agents must remain accountable for consequential actions.
Study anchor: E16-2609-035 · Agentic AI: tool access increases the need for accountable control

1. Anchor the magazine’s selected agentic-AI discussion without presenting simulations as verified incident rates or an industry proposal as a public regulator.
2. Explain the interaction of tool permissions, autonomy, model capability and deployment context. Separate assistive output from actions affecting rights, money or services.
3. Analyse privacy, prompt injection, goal misalignment, unreviewed tool use and unequal capacity to contest automated decisions.
4. Propose least privilege, sandboxing, human approval at consequential boundaries, independent tests, logs, rollback and incident reporting.
5. For AP services, retain human responsibility and accessible appeals; evaluate real service and safety outcomes rather than vendor claims or automation volume.

## 6. Housing on reclassified public land must protect water infrastructure and disaster safety.
Study anchor: E16-2609-051 · Nellore canal-land reclassification: retain the exclusions and buffer

1. Anchor the reported 3 September Nellore decision: 22.31 of 62.70 acres, active-canal/operational exclusions and reported 20-foot buffer.
2. Distinguish Cabinet approval, revenue reclassification, individual allotment, possession and safe construction; original survey and GO remain unread.
3. Analyse flood pathways, drainage, maintenance access, vulnerable households, boundary accuracy and possible competing public uses.
4. Propose transparent maps, site-specific hazard review, safeguarded access, consultation and grievance correction before irrevocable development.
5. For AP, reconcile housing inclusion with functioning public infrastructure. Do not assume a reported fixed buffer is scientifically sufficient for every location.

## 7. Government influencer outreach requires public-value safeguards, not merely digital reach.
Study anchor: E16-2609-053 · AP influencer empanelment: public information must remain identifiable

1. Anchor AP Cabinet policy reporting on 23 September, with one-year empanelment and conditional annual extensions, not verified executed contracts or payment rates.
2. Distinguish public information, advertising, political campaigning and independent creator speech; explain why paid sponsorship should be visible.
3. Analyse opaque selection, inflated metrics, misinformation risk, public spending, neutrality and chilling of legitimate criticism.
4. Propose transparent criteria, sponsorship labels, records, fact-checking, proportional grievance procedures and independent audit of contracts and outcomes.
5. Measure verified service awareness and uptake, not only views; respect criticism and avoid equating government disagreement with misinformation.

## 8. State investment policy should reward realised public value rather than approval totals.
Study anchor: E16-2609-054 · SIPB investment pipeline: proposals and jobs remain conditional

1. Anchor the reported ₹22,177.84 crore AP pipeline and 19,739 potential jobs; retain projected, not realised, status.
2. Map approval, land/clearances, financial closure, construction, production and sustained payroll. Do not invent a reconciled sector breakdown from conflicting numbers.
3. Analyse infrastructure, water and energy demand, environmental capacity, skill fit, regional distribution, incentive costs and contingent fiscal exposure.
4. Propose project-level dashboards, milestone-based support, transparent conditions, lawful clawbacks, worker safeguards and independently checked performance.
5. Conclude with realised investment, durable quality jobs and net public benefit; neither MoUs nor capital-per-projected-job ratios alone establish success.

# September 2026 · Original Mains answer frameworks

Eight frameworks for 150/250-word practice; not model claims of current law or exhaustive coverage. Preserve consultation and implementation stages.

## 1. Digital payments need a sustainable funding model without weakening inclusion.
Study anchor: E16-2609-002 · UPI MDR: merchant pricing is not a consumer transfer fee

1. Anchor the 15 September official FAQ and future 15 October commencement. Separate eligible merchant MDR from free ordinary payer/P2P use.
2. Explain the general 0.4% rate, ₹300 cap and exemptions/special categories without applying them indiscriminately to credit-linked products.
3. Analyse resilience, acceptance costs, merchant categorisation, pass-through incentives, competition and small-business margins. No-surcharge rules do not prove indirect economic incidence is impossible.
4. Propose transparent pricing, assisted classification, complaint resolution, reliable infrastructure and review using actual transaction/merchant outcomes.
5. For AP small merchants, test whether compliance knowledge, access and payment reliability improve rather than equating digital transaction growth with universal inclusion.

## 2. Renewable expansion is a system-integration challenge, not just a generation target.
Study anchor: E16-2609-008 · Green Energy Corridor III: integrate generation, transmission and storage

1. Anchor GEC III approval on 30 September: headline ₹1,86,405 crore, central support ₹54,082 crore, up to 135 GW evacuation and 50 GWh BESS through FY2032–33.
2. Distinguish power, stored energy, annual output, grid connection and operating service. Withhold unreconciled component sums rather than manufacturing precision.
3. Analyse transmission congestion, right-of-way, variable output, storage duration, degradation, safety and cost allocation.
4. Propose coordinated resource planning, competitive procurement where applicable, transparent milestones and performance monitoring tied to reliability and usable renewables.
5. For AP, link DISCOM finances, industrial/agricultural demand and project pipelines without asserting a new state allocation or already commissioned corridor.

## 3. Digital-market consumer protection requires enforceable design standards and informed choice.
Study anchor: E16-2609-010 · E-commerce amendment: notification now, commencement in 2027

1. Anchor the 9 September notification and 1 January 2027 commencement; contrast notification with operative effect.
2. Explain sponsored-ranking transparency, the lowest-prior-30-day discount reference and annual dark-pattern audit requirements.
3. Analyse information asymmetry, platform power, conflicted ranking, seller dependence, complaint evidence and audit credibility.
4. Propose independent enforcement capability, clear disclosures, accessible redress and proportionate sanctions while preserving genuine innovation and small-seller access.
5. For AP, assess whether consumer complaints and seller experiences improve; avoid assuming every online offer is unlawful or every self-audit is reliable.

## 4. Integrated land information must be accurate, contestable and inclusive.
Study anchor: E16-2609-018 · DILRMP3.0: a LandStack does not adjudicate every title

1. Anchor DILRMP 3.0’s September roadmap and primary anticipated ₹565.50 crore national outlay, retaining the prelaunch versus launch distinction.
2. Distinguish a 14-digit parcel identifier, map, mutation, registered transaction and adjudicated title; digitisation is not automatic conclusive ownership.
3. Analyse legacy discrepancies, survey quality, interdepartmental responsibility, privacy, exclusion and access barriers for women, tenants and customary users.
4. Propose notices, assisted claims, ground verification, audit trails, time-bound correction and meaningful appeal alongside interoperable technology.
5. For AP, measure fewer erroneous refusals, resolved disputes and reduced citizen burden rather than only map uploads or assuming a separate newly verified state scheme.

## 5. Public institutions deploying AI agents must remain accountable for consequential actions.
Study anchor: E16-2609-035 · Agentic AI: tool access increases the need for accountable control

1. Anchor the magazine’s selected agentic-AI discussion without presenting simulations as verified incident rates or an industry proposal as a public regulator.
2. Explain the interaction of tool permissions, autonomy, model capability and deployment context. Separate assistive output from actions affecting rights, money or services.
3. Analyse privacy, prompt injection, goal misalignment, unreviewed tool use and unequal capacity to contest automated decisions.
4. Propose least privilege, sandboxing, human approval at consequential boundaries, independent tests, logs, rollback and incident reporting.
5. For AP services, retain human responsibility and accessible appeals; evaluate real service and safety outcomes rather than vendor claims or automation volume.

## 6. Housing on reclassified public land must protect water infrastructure and disaster safety.
Study anchor: E16-2609-051 · Nellore canal-land reclassification: retain the exclusions and buffer

1. Anchor the reported 3 September Nellore decision: 22.31 of 62.70 acres, active-canal/operational exclusions and reported 20-foot buffer.
2. Distinguish Cabinet approval, revenue reclassification, individual allotment, possession and safe construction; original survey and GO remain unread.
3. Analyse flood pathways, drainage, maintenance access, vulnerable households, boundary accuracy and possible competing public uses.
4. Propose transparent maps, site-specific hazard review, safeguarded access, consultation and grievance correction before irrevocable development.
5. For AP, reconcile housing inclusion with functioning public infrastructure. Do not assume a reported fixed buffer is scientifically sufficient for every location.

## 7. Government influencer outreach requires public-value safeguards, not merely digital reach.
Study anchor: E16-2609-053 · AP influencer empanelment: public information must remain identifiable

1. Anchor AP Cabinet policy reporting on 23 September, with one-year empanelment and conditional annual extensions, not verified executed contracts or payment rates.
2. Distinguish public information, advertising, political campaigning and independent creator speech; explain why paid sponsorship should be visible.
3. Analyse opaque selection, inflated metrics, misinformation risk, public spending, neutrality and chilling of legitimate criticism.
4. Propose transparent criteria, sponsorship labels, records, fact-checking, proportional grievance procedures and independent audit of contracts and outcomes.
5. Measure verified service awareness and uptake, not only views; respect criticism and avoid equating government disagreement with misinformation.

## 8. State investment policy should reward realised public value rather than approval totals.
Study anchor: E16-2609-054 · SIPB investment pipeline: proposals and jobs remain conditional

1. Anchor the reported ₹22,177.84 crore AP pipeline and 19,739 potential jobs; retain projected, not realised, status.
2. Map approval, land/clearances, financial closure, construction, production and sustained payroll. Do not invent a reconciled sector breakdown from conflicting numbers.
3. Analyse infrastructure, water and energy demand, environmental capacity, skill fit, regional distribution, incentive costs and contingent fiscal exposure.
4. Propose project-level dashboards, milestone-based support, transparent conditions, lawful clawbacks, worker safeguards and independently checked performance.
5. Conclude with realised investment, durable quality jobs and net public benefit; neither MoUs nor capital-per-projected-job ratios alone establish success.

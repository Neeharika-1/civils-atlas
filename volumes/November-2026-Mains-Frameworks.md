# 1–7 November 2026 · Original Mains frameworks

Eight 150/250-word planning frameworks, not previous-year questions. This is a first-week selection, not full November coverage.

## 1. Official statistical modernisation must combine timeliness with credibility and privacy.
Study anchor: E18-2611-001 · VISTAR: a stronger statistical system needs trustworthy methods, not just more data

1. Anchor VISTAR’s ₹4,371.10 crore central-sector approval for 2026–31; distinguish a scheme from completed survey results or newly operative indices.
2. Explain the three components, planned 59 surveys, SSS 2.0 and 8th Economic Census of non-farm establishments; do not confuse it with the population Census.
3. Analyse representativeness, definitions, confidentiality, revisions, administrative/alternative data and transparent methodologies. AI can assist validation but also reproduce systematic errors.
4. For AP, connect trained district statistical teams, independent quality checks and interoperable records with usable local estimates and safeguarded respondent information.
5. Conclude with publication calendars, methodological disclosure, accessible datasets and measurable quality/timeliness, not only dashboards or survey counts.

## 2. Critical-mineral security requires processing capability as well as access to resources.
Study anchor: E18-2611-003 · Critical-mineral processing: support refining capability, not merely mine allocation

1. Anchor the ₹2,814 crore processing scheme, two lithium and two nickel plants, 30% eligible-capex support and mineral-specific ceilings.
2. Distinguish extraction, refining, precursors, cells, equipment and recycling; importing feedstock can coexist with domestic refining and residual dependencies.
3. Analyse technology, raw-material access, energy, water, waste, skills, offtake and supply concentration; least-support bidding must not displace credible delivery.
4. Propose milestone verification, transparent selection, lifecycle environmental oversight, diversified feedstock and research/industry partnerships.
5. For AP, identify transferable industrial-policy lessons without inventing a local selected beneficiary or treating future plants as commissioned capacity.

## 3. Storage support should procure useful flexibility rather than merely subsidise capacity headlines.
Study anchor: E18-2611-004 · Storage VGF: 92 GWh is supported future energy capacity, not power already available

1. Anchor ₹14,500 crore VGF and the prospective 92 GWh mix: 60 PSP, 30 BESS and 2 alternatives; distinguish ₹97,600 crore estimated investment.
2. Explain energy versus power, duration, efficiency, degradation, safety, water needs and actual services such as balancing/peak supply.
3. Analyse competitive tariff design, 40% BESS domestic-content verification, project risk, disposal/recycling and transparent public liabilities.
4. For AP DISCOMs, use demand/renewable profiles, available networks and credible availability obligations to choose projects and technology.
5. Conclude with useful delivered flexibility, lifecycle costs and safe operation; no inference that approved GWh are already commissioned or eliminate every supply constraint.

## 4. Special courts need both faster processes and a child-sensitive, fair justice system.
Study anchor: E18-2611-002 · Fast Track Special Courts: expand capacity without sacrificing fair process

1. Anchor the FTSC continuation through March 2031, ₹3,788.04 crore and expansion from 779 functional courts towards 850; distinguish exclusive POCSO courts.
2. Map investigation, forensics, prosecution, legal aid, judicial vacancies, witness support and vulnerable-witness spaces as a complete service chain.
3. Analyse why disposal speed alone can conceal poor investigation, distress, unfair procedure, unreliable evidence or repeated adjournments elsewhere.
4. Propose dedicated competent personnel, accessible legal/psychosocial support, reasoned decisions, coordinated scheduling and independent qualitative assessment.
5. For AP, allocate capacity by pendency and need rather than assuming a fixed uniform court entitlement for every district; preserve fair-trial and child-protection principles.

## 5. Digital complaint handling should increase accessibility without substituting automation for remedies.
Study anchor: E18-2611-007 · Telecom grievance reform: 24-hour support is not 24 hours of human staffing

1. Anchor the 2 November telecom release; distinguish announced final amendments from separately uninspected commencement/exemption clauses.
2. Explain round-the-clock support with at least 16 hours of human help, multiple accessible channels and distinct 15-day filing/decision periods.
3. Analyse language, disability access, digital exclusion, AI errors, wrongful closures and the burden of shorter appeal windows.
4. Use feedback, quarterly oversight, consumer participation and audits to identify recurring defects; preserve human escalation and reasoned responses.
5. For AP, evaluate actual resolution, timeliness and vulnerable consumers’ experience rather than equating app downloads, closed tickets or raw satisfaction scores with success.

## 6. Conservation beyond protected areas must combine ecological outcomes and legitimate local governance.
Study anchor: E18-2611-014 · I-OECMs: conserve beyond protected areas without changing ownership

1. Anchor the 3 November I-OECM guideline event, reported on 4 November, and distinguish recognition from a protected-area notification or transfer of ownership.
2. Explain boundaries, non-overlap, institutions, review, sustained in-situ outcomes and monitoring; full primary guideline/project texts were not independently inspected.
3. Analyse local rights, existing uses, financing, consent, biodiversity significance and the danger of nominal or double-counted conservation.
4. For AP, connect the named Srisailam–Cumbum demonstration landscape, local biodiversity institutions, tanks and ecosystems without blanket parcel-level recognition claims.
5. Conclude with transparent decisions, accessible grievance routes and credible indicators, not only recognised-area totals or conservation labels.

## 7. Public-land monetisation should demonstrate public value beyond auction premiums.
Study anchor: E18-2611-020 · RINL land auction: successful bids are not independently verified settled receipts

1. Anchor RINL Phase II on 3 November: 114 parcels and ₹218 crore successful bids; preserve the distinction between bids, settlement and realised receipts.
2. Distinguish surplus-land monetisation from sale of the operating steel enterprise; note Phase I’s October date rather than counting it twice in November.
3. Analyse title, public uses, infrastructure, planning conditions, bidder access, market competition, benchmark valuation and distribution of benefits/costs.
4. Propose transparent surveys/terms, appropriate safeguards, audited settlement and disclosure of how proceeds support lawful public/enterprise purposes.
5. For Visakhapatnam, assess long-term urban and industrial implications; a premium over reserve is evidence of one comparison, not a conclusive welfare assessment.

## 8. Consecutive cyclones require integrated relief, lifeline restoration and trustworthy loss assessment.
Study anchor: E18-2611-021 · Murasu: distinguish confirmed coastal crossing from preliminary loss snapshots

1. Anchor Murasu’s 3 November reported crossing near Ongole after Arnab; separate forecasts, observed reports, preliminary loss snapshots and final assessments.
2. Map evacuation, shelters, health, food, drinking water, safe electricity, communications, roads and public warning as linked functions.
3. Distinguish ₹37 crore state emergency allocation, regular devolution, private insurance and requested central assistance; none automatically establishes final household receipt.
4. Compare source-specific crop/horticulture estimates and timestamped feeder/tower repair data; do not merge conflicting figures or treat an asset percentage as universal service recovery.
5. For AP, combine accessible verification/appeals and livelihood rehabilitation with resilient siting, drainage, ecosystems and transparent reconstruction expenditure.

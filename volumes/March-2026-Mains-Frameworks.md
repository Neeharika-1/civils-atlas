# March 2026 · Eight original Mains frameworks

Original planning aids, not official model answers or predictions. Facts, static connections and proposed reforms remain distinct.

## How should India manage external vulnerability during an energy shock?

**Study reference:** E10-2603-002

**Dated anchor:** RBI released preliminary Q3 FY 2025–26 data on 2 March.

1. Anchor the 2 March release: Q3 CAD US$13.2 billion/1.3% of GDP; identify the reference quarter as October–December 2025.
2. Map transmission: import bill, freight/insurance, input costs, inflation, exchange-rate pressure and external financing.
3. Explain cushions: services, remittances, reserves and diversified supply; each has different limits and time horizons.
4. Counterpoint: suppressing all price signals can shift costs to budgets, while abrupt pass-through can harm vulnerable households.
5. Recommend targeted support, transparent contingency planning, efficiency and stable external-financing policy. Distinguish BoP-basis reserve flows from valuation-driven stock changes.

**Evidence caution:** A BoP-basis reserve flow is not identical to the change in the published reserve stock including valuation effects. These are preliminary estimates.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the institution, financing/rights safeguard and measurable outcome rather than merely restating the proposal.

## Examine adoptive-maternity protection through substantive equality.

**Dated anchor:** The Supreme Court decided Hamsaanandini Nanduri on 17 March.

1. Use the 17 March ruling’s removal of the adopted child’s three-month age restriction; preserve the specified 12-week entitlement.

5. Propose a wider shared-care framework, while distinguishing the Court’s paternity-leave suggestion from an enacted universal entitlement.

**Evidence caution:** Do not convert this ruling into 26 weeks for all adoptive mothers or a newly enacted nationwide paternity entitlement.

## Can transparent investment screening reconcile capital access and economic security?

**Study reference:** E10-2603-009

**Dated anchor:** DPIIT issued Press Note 2 on 15 March.

1. Anchor DPIIT Press Note2, 15 March, and its explicit dependence on a FEMA notification for effect.
2. Explain indirect ownership, entity-specific thresholds, aggregated interests and ultimate effective control.
3. Benefits: predictable rules can reduce uncertainty for diversified funds while preserving review of controlled investments.
4. Risks: layered entities, information asymmetry, discretionary delay and weak enforcement can defeat both investment and security aims.
5. Recommend clear reporting, auditable beneficial-ownership information, reasoned decisions and timelines; retain sectoral caps and do not use a percentage as the sole test.

**Evidence caution:** Do not assert that every sub-10% interest qualifies for unrestricted automatic entry, or that the press-note date itself proves legal commencement.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the institution, financing/rights safeguard and measurable outcome rather than merely restating the proposal.

## How can JJM move from counting connections to assuring rural water services?

**Study reference:** E10-2603-026

**Dated anchor:** The Cabinet approved JJM restructuring and extension on 10 March.

1. Anchor the December2028 extension and separate total outlay ₹8.69 lakh crore from central assistance ₹3.59 lakh crore.
2. Map source sustainability, treatment, distribution, household availability, quality testing and repair response.
3. Use Sujalam Bharat mapping, Jal Arpan and GP/O&M certification as accountability mechanisms rather than outcomes already proved.
4. In AP, connect water-stressed sources, local maintenance finance and village capacity; avoid treating a tap as proof of year-round safe supply.
5. Recommend service-level disclosure, inclusive community oversight and maintenance support. Measure downtime, quality and household reliability, not only assets built.

**Evidence caution:** ₹8.69 lakh crore is total outlay, not entirely Union assistance or spending already incurred. Reported connections and modelled health benefits are not identical evidence.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the institution, financing/rights safeguard and measurable outcome rather than merely restating the proposal.

## Assess the implementation challenge in India’s 2035 climate commitments.

**Study reference:** E10-2603-027

**Dated anchor:** The Cabinet approved India’s 2031–35 NDC on 25 March. March is the editorial coverage index; the web edition was posted 30 April. Event dates are distinguished in the text.

1. State the targets accurately: 47% GDP-intensity reduction versus2005, 60% non-fossil installed capacity, and the specified forest/tree carbon sink.
2. Explain intensity versus absolute emissions and capacity versus generation; non-fossil includes more than renewables.
3. Map implementation needs across power networks/storage, industrial efficiency, finance, livelihoods and ecological restoration.
4. Counterpoint: nominal capacity additions may underdeliver without grid integration; forest quantity can hide ecological-quality problems.
5. Recommend sectoral milestones and transparent monitoring. Cabinet approval is not independently verified UNFCCC submission, and the 2070 net-zero goal is distinct.

**Evidence caution:** Do not call this a 47% absolute emissions cut, 60% renewable generation, or proof of the UNFCCC submission date. Non-fossil is broader than renewables.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the institution, financing/rights safeguard and measurable outcome rather than merely restating the proposal.

## How can environmental clearance be timely without weakening scrutiny?

**Study reference:** E10-2603-034

**Dated anchor:** March’s magazine discusses a draft amendment to the 2006 EIA framework. March is the editorial coverage index; the web edition was posted 30 April. Event dates are distinguished in the text.

1. Anchor the draft standing-state-body proposal as a response to institutional vacancies and delays, not verified final law.
2. Explain the scheduled-project/category framework; explicitly reject a universal ₹50-crore EIA test.
3. Benefits: continuity, digital routing and clear appraisal responsibilities can reduce avoidable uncertainty.
4. Risks: poor expertise, rushed public consultation, conflicts and deadline-driven rubber-stamping can undermine environmental legitimacy.
5. Recommend adequate capacity, transparent reasons, participation and compliance monitoring. Institutional delay should not be treated as automatic environmental approval.

**Evidence caution:** Reject the article’s blanket claim that all projects above ₹50 crore require EIA. There is no single universal ₹50-crore test. This is a draft, not verified final law.

## Explain notified social status, legal protection and evidence in the AP-linked SC-status ruling.

**Study reference:** E10-2603-047

**Dated anchor:** The Supreme Court dismissed the AP-linked appeal on 24 March.

1. Anchor Chinthada Anand, 24 March, and paragraph3 of the SC Order1950; distinguish the challenged HC order’s 30 April 2025 date.
2. Explain that SC-status-dependent benefits must follow the notified legal framework and the Court’s treatment of the claimant’s facts.
3. Preserve the explicit ST distinction: no equivalent religion-based exclusion; the Court describes a fact-sensitive tribal-identity assessment.
4. Analyse ordinary IPC allegations separately; case-specific evidentiary findings do not extinguish universal criminal-law protection or deny persistent social discrimination.
5. Conclude with careful status verification, due process and avoidance of sweeping generalisations. This is an answer framework, not individual legal advice.

**Evidence caution:** Do not generalise this SC rule to automatic loss of ST status, all minority rights or immunity for violence. The IPC findings were case-specific, not a general bar on complaints.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the institution, financing/rights safeguard and measurable outcome rather than merely restating the proposal.

# October 2026 · Original Mains answer frameworks

Eight 150/250-word planning frameworks; not previous-year questions or completeness/current-law certification. Source stages and disputed figures must be retained.

## 1. Monetary policy should respond to second-round supply-shock risks without confusing forecasts with outcomes.
Study anchor: E17-2610-001 · October MPC: a rate-hike consensus is not unanimity on stance

1. Anchor the 7 October decision: repo 5.50%, SDF 5.25%, MSF/Bank Rate 5.75%, 25 bp hike. Distinguish unanimous rate vote from two neutral-stance preferences.
2. Use primary prose growth 7.1%/CPI 5.2% forecasts and document PRS/chart disagreements; do not invent a merged official series or claim realised annual results.
3. Explain how expectations, pricing and financial conditions transmit policy when food/energy shocks originate on the supply side.
4. Analyse growth, inclusion, credit costs, communication and time lags; combine monetary discipline with appropriate supply-side/fiscal action rather than assuming rates produce missing commodities.
5. For AP, trace household, MSME, farm and state-borrowing effects with clear transmission assumptions and dated evidence.

## 2. GST process reform must combine taxpayer trust, legal clarity and credible enforcement.
Study anchor: E17-2610-005 · 57th GST Council: distinguish recommendations from enacted powers

1. Anchor the 8 October Council recommendations, not an assumed blanket legal commencement. Distinguish Council advice, statutory amendments, rules and portal changes.
2. Explain proposed arrest/prosecution/penalty changes and simplified registration/refunds while preserving thresholds, denominators and different proposed dates.
3. Analyse working-capital blockage, arbitrary notices, hearing rights, fraud risks and system errors; automation can reduce discretion but also amplify incorrect data.
4. Propose clear notices, accessible objections, reasoned decisions, transparent risk models, training and measurable refund/complaint outcomes.
5. For AP taxpayers, avoid promising immediate legal relief from a press release; verify operative instruments and maintain multilingual assisted compliance.

## 3. Social-assistance expansion requires coverage, adequacy and reliable delivery to be assessed separately.
Study anchor: E17-2610-016 · NSAP continuation: new disability eligibility needs implementation follow-through

1. Anchor the 14 October NSAP continuation/renaming, ₹53,428 crore 2026–31, and the stated three-month guideline implementation window.
2. Explain approved disability benchmark 40%, eligibility from birth subject to other conditions, and ₹375/₹625 central assistance by age; distinguish state top-ups.
3. Analyse exclusion errors, certification burden, payment failures, care costs and adequacy. A larger eligibility pool does not prove every household receives support.
4. Propose assisted enrolment, portable correction, time-bound grievances, accessible channels and transparent payment monitoring with privacy safeguards.
5. For AP, connect central support with operative state schemes rather than replacing local pension figures with the central amount.

## 4. Transmission expansion should reconcile fair land compensation with timely public infrastructure.
Study anchor: E17-2610-023 · Transmission RoW: verify voltage, scope and market-rate procedure

1. Anchor 6 October guidelines and their 66 kV+ ISTS/state-policy scope, not every local distribution line.
2. Distinguish right of way, full acquisition, tower footprint, corridor diminution, crop/tree damage, circle rate and market value.
3. Explain three-valuers/median-normalisation process and administrative appeal; do not interpret administrative finality as a blanket bar on constitutional judicial review.
4. Analyse transparent valuation, upfront payment, landowner identification, access to contestation, project costs and time-bound resolution.
5. For AP, verify applicable state policy, project category and site conditions before applying a percentage or asserting an actual entitlement.

## 5. Examination credibility depends on accountable institutions as well as secure technology.
Study anchor: E17-2610-029 · NTA committee report: security technology cannot replace accountable institutions

1. Anchor the parliamentary committee’s recommendations on NTA, not independently verified new operational rules for every examination.
2. Analyse staffing, vendor procurement, paper security, question quality, coaching inequities, language, travel and disability access.
3. Explain what CBT, biometrics, CCTV and AI can and cannot solve; include false positives, system outages, privacy and candidate grievance rights.
4. Propose permanent specialised capacity, independent audit, transparent vendor sanctions, secure content, contingency plans, counselling and reasoned remedies.
5. For AP candidates, measure equitable service quality and timely correction, not only centres or surveillance devices installed.

## 6. Foreign-university facilitation should improve quality and equitable access rather than merely attract brands.
Study anchor: E17-2610-053 · AP foreign-university policy: approval is not an operational campus

1. Anchor AP’s 7 October Cabinet policy approval; the full policy and campus-specific UGC permissions were not independently inspected.
2. Distinguish state facilitation, land/incentives, central regulation, qualification recognition, construction and an operational academic campus.
3. Analyse fees, faculty, research links, academic autonomy, student protection, regional access and the opportunity cost of public support.
4. Propose transparent agreements, clear exit/teach-out safeguards, quality monitoring, scholarship access and credible disclosure of fiscal/noncash support.
5. Evaluate actual research, learning and inclusion outcomes; do not repeat “no financial burden” as an independently proven zero-cost result or promise automatic admissions.

## 7. Education transfers should be judged by reliable inclusion and learning outcomes, not sanction totals alone.
Study anchor: E17-2610-055 · Thalliki Vandanam expansion: new inclusions and second-phase payments differ

1. Anchor AP’s 7 October expansion: 58,601 new inclusions, coverage 68,05,791, and ₹10,208.69 crore revised sanction. Distinguish 3,29,201 second-phase beneficiaries.
2. Map eligibility, records, administrative sanction, treasury release, bank credit and actual receipt; separate student counts from mother/guardian payees.
3. Analyse missing documentation, migration, disability, digital exclusion, duplicate records and banking failures alongside educational participation.
4. Propose assisted corrections, clear exclusion reasons, privacy-protected dashboards, timely appeals and independent sample verification.
5. Link support with attendance, school quality, nutrition and learning; do not assume a financial transfer alone guarantees educational progress.

## 8. Cyclone resilience requires timestamped warnings, safe response and accountable recovery.
Study anchor: E17-2610-063 · Cyclone Arnab: separate an evolving forecast from confirmed landfall

1. Anchor Arnab’s 28 October landfall reporting, distinguishing the early IMD forecast from later completion reports and withholding unaudited final losses.
2. Map wind, rain, storm surge, inland flooding and electrical hazards; explain why the centre’s predicted track is not the complete impact footprint.
3. Analyse evacuation, accessible shelters, fisher livelihoods, health, water, power, telecom and interstate assistance, with attention to vulnerable groups.
4. Propose clear warning updates, local exposure maps, safe restoration, verified damage records, layered finance and resilient infrastructure/planning.
5. For AP, combine rapid relief with ecosystem protection and long-term risk reduction; do not infer completed restoration from assistance announcements or put November events into October.

## Can temporary SEZ–DTA concessions support manufacturing without distorting competition?

**Study reference:** E11-2604-007

**Dated anchor:** The notified concession framework applies 1 April 2026–31 March 2027.

1. Anchor 1 April 2026–31 March 2027, the 20% value-addition condition and the 30% cap with its historical FOB-export denominator.
2. Explain the benefit: capacity utilisation, employment continuity and lower unit costs during external disruption.
3. Counterpoint: DTA producers may face uneven conditions; export benefits must not be claimed twice.
4. Assess eligibility certification, audit, product exclusions and the difference between a concession and duty-free clearance.
5. Recommend transparent temporary review using additional production, jobs and genuine compliance, not only domestic sales volume.

**Evidence caution:** The cap is not 30% of current-year total production, and value addition is not identical to the duty rate.

**Conclusion test:** Identify the responsible institution, the financing/rights safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## How should renewable-energy progress be measured beyond installed capacity?

**Study reference:** E11-2604-030

**Dated anchor:** The issue reviews renewable-capacity and electricity-transition reports. April is the editorial coverage index; public web posting was 22 May. Event/release dates can be earlier.

1. Define GWcapacity separately from period-specific electricity inTWh/billionunits.
2. Distinguish renewable from non-fossil and capacity share from generation share.

5. Recommend reporting reliable generation, emissions, cost and service outcomes alongside capacity. Reject unverified 51.5% demand claims derived from a capacity statistic.

**Evidence caution:** Do not call a capacity ranking the same thing as global renewable electricity production rank or equate 51.5% capacity with energy demand met.

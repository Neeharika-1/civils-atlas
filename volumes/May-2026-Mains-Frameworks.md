# May 2026 · Eight original Mains frameworks

Original planning aids, not official answers or predictions. Anchor each argument in its source/date and preserve open implementation questions.

## Central labour reform needs both clarity and workplace enforceability.

**Study reference:** E12-2605-002

**Dated anchor:** Central Rules were notified on 8 May.

1. Anchor 8 May central rules. Explain appropriate-government jurisdiction rather than applying every central rule to every AP workplace.
2. Use inspected examples: 48 weekly hours and written consent, transport, lighting and emergency safeguards for women’s night work.
3. Link wage-setting to floor/minimum distinctions, CPI-IW adjustment and reliable contractor-payment chains.
4. Counterpoint: digital records and permission clauses do not remove coercion, under-reporting or weak inspections.
5. Recommend worker-accessible grievances, trained inspectors and outcome checks of actual wages, hours and safety.

**Evidence caution:** Do not universalise every central rule to every AP establishment or equate 48 weekly hours with an unconditional 12-hour daily norm.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## Design portable social protection for platform workers.

**Study reference:** E12-2605-003

**Dated anchor:** Selected Social Security Central Rules were independently inspected.

1. Anchor the inspected registration/eligibility provisions and 90/120-day distinction.
2. Explain cumulative aggregator-days: a calendar date with three aggregators can count three times. Do not promise every registrant every salaried benefit.
3. Assess portability, fragmented earnings, intermittent work and workers’ ability to inspect/correct platform records.
4. Counterpoint: badly designed thresholds can exclude vulnerable low-engagement workers while registration counts exaggerate coverage.
5. Recommend clear scheme eligibility, sustainable financing, accessible appeal and reporting of benefits actually received.

**Evidence caution:** Do not substitute 120 distinct calendar days for the rule’s cumulative counting, or promise automatic EPF/ESI payments to every registrant.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## Emergency credit guarantees should support viable firms without disguising fiscal risk.

**Study reference:** E12-2605-007

**Dated anchor:** The 5 May approval targets temporary liquidity pressures linked to West Asia disruption.

1. Anchor 5 May ECLGS 5.0, standard-account eligibility and credit guaranteed through NCGTC.
2. Explain 20% of peak utilised Q4 working capital, ₹100 crore cap and distinct airline rules. Guarantee is not a debt waiver.
3. Analyse how temporary liquidity support can preserve supply chains and jobs during external shocks.
4. Counterpoint: guarantees create contingent public liabilities and may weaken appraisal if lenders assume no residual risk.
5. Recommend additionality checks, transparent default/recovery reporting, sector exposure limits and credible exit.

**Evidence caution:** Do not apply 20% to the sanctioned but unused limit, equate a guarantee with a waiver, or count targeted credit as paid expenditure.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## A circular economy cannot be built through recycling certificates alone.

**Study reference:** E12-2605-038

**Dated anchor:** The issue discusses industry proposals for stronger coordination. May is the editorial coverage index; the public edition was posted on 30 June. Event/release dates can differ.

1. Anchor the issue’s coordination proposal as an advocacy position, not an already-created ministry.
2. Define the hierarchy: reduce, durable design, repair, reuse, refurbish and recover materials.
3. For AP municipalities, connect source segregation, EPR, worker safety and financially viable end-markets.
4. Counterpoint: weak traceability can encourage false recovery claims; incineration and material recycling are not identical.
5. Recommend auditable material flows, circular public procurement, repair ecosystems and measures of displaced virgin extraction.

**Evidence caution:** Do not equate waste-to-energy incineration with material recycling or repeat a projected 2050 market value as present output.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## Can pronatalist support respect reproductive autonomy?

**Study reference:** E12-2605-062

**Dated anchor:** The 16 May BusinessLine report records new incentive figures.

1. Anchor 16 May announced ₹30,000/₹40,000 third/fourth-child figures; operating GO and payments remain unverified.
2. Distinguish sub-replacement TFR from immediate population decline, and national averages from state age structures.
3. Consider childcare, housing, employment security, maternal health and gendered care burdens alongside cash.
4. Counterpoint: incentives may have limited durable fertility effects and become coercive if attached to public targets or denial of contraception.
5. Recommend voluntary rights-based support, transparent eligibility, health safeguards and long-term evaluation rather than birth quotas.

**Evidence caution:** Do not say every eligible family was already paid or that a political announcement proves a final legally claimable entitlement.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## Why should health policy look beyond insurance enrolment?

**Study reference:** E12-2605-048

**Dated anchor:** The issue describes the 2025 NSS 80th-round health survey. May is the editorial coverage index; the public edition was posted on 30 June. Event/release dates can differ.

1. Anchor 2025 survey reference year and separate morbidity, hospitalisation, insurance and expenditure denominators.
2. Explain why 57.9% rural/64.6% urban hospitalisation shares cannot be pooled without weights.
3. Assess exclusions, outpatient medicines, provider costs, referral delays and lost earnings that affect financial protection.
4. For AP, link primary/public facility readiness to treatment completion and claims rather than only enrolment cards.
5. Recommend independent financial-protection measures, quality safeguards and accessible redress; do not infer clinical prevalence from self-reporting alone.

**Evidence caution:** A rise in insurance coverage does not prove catastrophic spending disappeared; symptom reporting is not the same as clinical prevalence.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.

## How can the Andhra education model improve inclusion and learning together?

**Study reference:** E12-2605-063

**Dated anchor:** TNIE reports the 20 May education review on 21 May.

1. Anchor 20 May review reported 21 May. Treat 100% enrolment and 10% government-school growth as goals, not achievements.
2. Distinguish never-enrolled, dropout, attendance, grade completion and foundational-learning measures.
3. Connect GFLN, teacher capacity, accessible infrastructure and migrant-family support; devices alone are not pedagogy.
4. Counterpoint: dashboard pressure can create inflated enrolment or conceal absence; more government-school admissions do not automatically prove higher learning.
5. Recommend independent learning checks, safe infrastructure, teacher support and follow-up until children attend and progress.

**Evidence caution:** Do not report 100% enrolment as achieved or equate government-school market share growth with system-wide learning gains.

**Conclusion test:** Identify a responsible institution, an implementation safeguard and a measurable outcome.
